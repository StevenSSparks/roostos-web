The idea

A device that's actually yours

Most gadgets are sealed boxes running someone else's software. RoostOS is the opposite: you take a $20–$60 board that already has a screen, a keyboard, WiFi, and a battery, and you turn it into something you built and you understand — in code you can read.

It began as a question: how much real "computer" can you pack into a microcontroller? That grew into two projects. One is a handheld AI communicator — a pocket QWERTY device that chats with Claude, GPT, Gemini, or your own local model over WiFi. The other is a "data center" in a microcontroller — a single chip serving its own WiFi, DNS, real HTTPS website, and an SSH shell.

Both are open source (MIT), both flash from your laptop with one command, and neither needs a soldering iron. Where a lot of maker projects need a 3D printer, custom PCBs, or special gear, this is 100% off-the-shelf: the simple Roost Server runs on a board for under $20, and the whole Communicator handheld is under $100. Whether you're a total beginner who just wants a cool gadget or an expert who wants to hack on the firmware, this is for you. Here's the whole story — and a step-by-step invitation to build your own.

What makes it unique

Not your average ESP32 sketch

The Communicator packs a surprising amount of "real device" into a pocket board:

🤖

Bring your own AI

Chat with Claude, GPT, Gemini, or a local Ollama model. Pick the active one in Settings — it verifies the key answers before switching, so you're never stuck on a dead provider.

🗺️

Maps & real directions

Ask "a good steakhouse in Clayton?" or "driving directions to the Gateway Arch" and it searches the web, draws the map, and gives turn-by-turn directions — distance, time, and numbered steps — from your block to across the country.

💬

A real conversation

It remembers the context across turns — ask a follow-up, say "no", use "it" or "there" — the device replays the chat so the AI actually keeps the thread. The new button starts fresh.

🔐

SSH into a handheld

Turn on the SSH server and ssh roost@<device> from any laptop — you drop straight into the same chat, and can configure everything. A pocket device you can log into.

📶

2.4 GHz WiFi

ESP32 boards are 2.4 GHz only — join a 2.4 GHz network (most routers broadcast both). The T-Deck's WiFi antenna clips onto a tiny U.FL connector; seat it for a strong signal.

⚙️

Configure any way

On the touchscreen, over USB-C, over the network, or via a browser config builder. Flash with just WiFi, then paste your API key over the shell — no rebuild.

You can build this too

From box to working device

No soldering, no prior hardware experience. If you can copy/paste a terminal command and plug in a USB-C cable, you're about 20 minutes from a device booting your own code.

STEP 1

Order a board

Get a LILYGO T-Deck Plus (Communicator) or any ESP32 board (Roost) — online or in person at Micro Center or GigaParts. Screen, WiFi and battery are built in; nothing to wire.

STEP 2

Get an AI key

For the Communicator, grab an API key — Anthropic (Claude), OpenAI, or Gemini (free tier). Or run a local Ollama model with no key at all.

STEP 3

Set your values

Use the browser config builder (or copy secrets.example.h) to enter your 2.4 GHz WiFi + key. It runs entirely in your browser — nothing is uploaded.

STEP 4

Flash & go

Install PlatformIO (brew install platformio), plug in USB-C, run pio run -e app -t upload, and watch your software boot. Start chatting.

Where to get the parts

Everything is off-the-shelf and USB-C — order online or walk into a store. No soldering iron, no breadboard.

🐤 Communicator board — LILYGO T-Deck Plus $89.99 at Micro Center (SKU 942391) — QWERTY, 2.8″ screen, WiFi, GPS & battery built in. Also at GigaParts. Nothing to wire. 💵 Roost Server board — Seeed XIAO ESP32-S3 kit $18.95 at GigaParts (SKU ZSE-0005). Any ESP32-S3 works — this is the easy under-$20 pick. Micro Center ESP32 dev boards, USB-C cables, maker gear — in-store & online. GigaParts LILYGO T-Deck, ESP32 boards, antennas & accessories — online or call 1-256-384-1008. Anthropic (Claude) key console.anthropic.com → API Keys (recommended default). Gemini key (free tier) aistudio.google.com/app/apikey — or use OpenAI / local Ollama.

📶 Heads-up on WiFi: ESP32 is 2.4 GHz only. Pick a 2.4 GHz SSID (most home routers broadcast 2.4 & 5 GHz — use the 2.4). On the T-Deck Plus, make sure the WiFi antenna is snapped onto its U.FL connector, or the signal will be weak.
